When the power goes out, someone has to know.

Lifeline watches the outlet behind a patient's oxygen, ventilator or dialysis machine. The moment it goes dark, the right people hear, one after another, until someone is on the way.

People on Medicare in Forrest and Lamar counties use powered medical equipment at home.

Oxygen concentrators, ventilators, home dialysis, feeding and suction pumps: they need the outlet. Across Mississippi the same federal data counts 43,011 people, and at the peak of Winter Storm Fern more than 180,000 Mississippi utility customers were without power.

Sources: HHS emPOWER, Oct 2026 (43,011). SuperTalk Mississippi, Jan 26 2026 (180,000+). Medicare (fee-for-service and Medicare Advantage) only; Medicaid-only and privately insured patients are not counted.

Sixteen homes go dark at once. Your nurse knows who to call first.

Every home ranked by how much backup is left. One tap to respond, call or get directions. Built for the worst night of the year, readable from across the room.

It warns you the night before the storm.

  • Storm Watch

    National Weather Service warnings over every home on the map, and a call-first list of the patients with the least backup.

  • County view

    Emergency management sees who has no power and how long their backup lasts, with no phone numbers.

  • The record you owe

    Every outage, alert and response time, exported for your emergency-preparedness file.

Federal rules already make this your job. Lifeline makes it doable at 3 AM.
