When the power goes out, someone has to know.

Lifeline watches the outlet behind a patient's oxygen, ventilator or dialysis machine. The moment it goes dark, the right people hear, one after another, until someone is on the way.

HHS emPOWER, Nov 2026

People on Medicare across the United States rely on powered medical equipment at home.

Oxygen concentrators, ventilators, home dialysis, feeding and suction pumps: they need the outlet. Outages are getting longer: in 2024 the average US electricity customer went 11 hours without power, nearly twice the yearly average of the decade before, and major events such as hurricanes caused 80% of those hours.

Sources: HHS emPOWER REST Service, Nov 2026, state and territory counts summed (3,115,917). U.S. Energy Information Administration, Today in Energy, Dec 1 2025 (11 hours, 80%). Medicare (fee-for-service and Medicare Advantage) only; Medicaid-only and privately insured patients are not counted, so the real number is higher.

  • Where we listened

    Hattiesburg, Mississippi, where the first prototype was built. Lifeline is for home health agencies anywhere in the United States.

  • What we heard

    In November 2026 we talked to staff at three Hattiesburg care providers: Covenant Caregivers (non-medical home care), Windham House of Hattiesburg (nursing home) and Alden Pointe (assisted living). None had a system like this, and all three said it would help. Separately, by phone, an on-call triage nurse at a home health agency told us they have no direct alert when a patient's power goes out; they hear about it only when someone else tells them.

Ten homes go dark at once. Your nurse knows who to call first.

Every home ranked by how much backup is left. One tap to respond, call or get directions. Built for the worst night of the year, readable from across the room.

The family and the county see the same night, each from their side.

The family gets a text and a link, not an app.

The first person on the list hears within seconds. The link opens a page with the time left, the clock time it runs out, a 911 button, and one tap that tells everyone else they are on the way.

The county sees the list, without phone numbers.

Emergency management gets every home without power, most urgent first, read only. The storm warning is said once, not on every row, and only the homes under 15 minutes are red.

It warns you the night before the storm.

  • Storm Watch

    National Weather Service warnings over every home on the map, and a call-first list of the patients with the least backup.

  • County view

    Emergency management sees who has no power and how long their backup lasts, with no phone numbers.

  • The record you owe

    Every outage, alert and response time, exported for your emergency-preparedness file.
